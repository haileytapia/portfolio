# Modernizing Microsoft Learn contributor guidance
{: .no_toc }

[Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/) hosts documentation for hundreds of products across Azure, Visual Studio, Windows, Linux, and more. As these documentation sets evolved, so did the needs of their contributors, who had to keep pace with changing customer expectations, emerging technologies, and the growing role of AI in how people discover and use information.

## Three workstreams

I was part of a five-person working group commissioned by leadership to define the foundation for the next generation of guidance. Our group focused on three interrelated aspects of how documentation is created and organized.

### Content types

We examined how documentation should be classified and structured based on its purpose, audience, and role in the customer journey. This work covered content-type definitions, metadata, templates, lifecycle considerations, and distinctions between different forms of documentation, including generated reference content, "What's New" updates, and release notes.

### Information flow

We explored how documentation can guide customers across the full product journey, from initial awareness and evaluation to implementation, operation, and product changes. This work included identifying canonical paths, establishing linking expectations, and considering how tooling could help connect related content and reduce dead ends.

### Information architecture

We developed principles for organizing documentation around customer tasks and scenarios. This work considered navigation, TOC structures, landing pages, hubs, and labeling, with an emphasis on making information easier to discover and understand while supporting consistent organization across documentation sets.

## Impact

Across the three workstreams, we documented best practices, examined edge cases, considered implementation options, identified tooling needs, and outlined recommendations for next steps. Rather than immediately codifying new rules, we defined the principles, reasoning, and practical considerations that could inform future contributor guidance.

Together, this work established a foundation for modernizing contributor guidance across Microsoft Learn. It provided a more coherent basis for future standards and scalable documentation practices, including how content is classified, connected, and organized to support both human readers and AI-mediated discovery.
